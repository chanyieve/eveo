How it works

Rendering. Nothing here is an image file. The paper is divided into a coarse grid, and each cell stores water, two suspended pigments and two dried pigments. Every step, water creeps into neighbouring paper and evaporates (fastest at the edge of a puddle), while suspended pigment is carried from deep water toward shallow water and gradually fixes to the paper. That outward drift is what leaves the darker rim on a dried stroke.

Colour is mixed subtractively: each pigment absorbs light according to how much of it sits in a cell, so blue over ochre deepens toward a grey-green instead of averaging. Ochre is treated as the heavier pigment: it travels less and settles into the grain.

Limits. The simulation runs on the CPU at a reduced resolution, then is scaled up, so edges are soft rather than crisp. If a frame takes too long the grid is coarsened automatically. On a slow device the wash dries more slowly instead of skipping steps. Resizing or rotating keeps the painting but may crop it.

Pause holds the water still. You can keep adding pigment, and it starts moving again when you resume.
